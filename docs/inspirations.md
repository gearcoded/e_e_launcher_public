# Inspirations and origins of e_e launcher

e_e launcher grew from familiar interfaces, memories of older software, and experiments that did not always turn out as planned. This page is a personal account of those influences — **not a list of third-party code or assets used by the project**. Actual external components and artwork are documented separately in [Third-party licenses](../THIRD_PARTY_LICENSES.md) and [Asset provenance](../ASSET_PROVENANCE.md).

Many of the features described here exist in other applications too. The projects below are the ones I personally encountered and remembered, rather than a claim that any particular company invented a feature.

## Where it began: TSF Launcher

**TSF Launcher** was the strongest early influence on e_e launcher. When I moved to another phone, I wanted to keep the behaviors I was comfortable with instead of losing them. I see e_e launcher as carrying some of those ideas forward in my own way, without any connection to the original team or reuse of its code or artwork.

Among the ideas I wanted to bring into my own launcher were:

- Dragging shortcuts out of folders.
- A vertical side dock.
- Cloud, snow, and tumbleweed-like environment effects.
- A cascading twist-style workspace transition, although I also remember seeing a related effect in Compiz.
- Transparent dialog windows and the ability to restart the launcher from its settings.
- A visual marker for newly installed applications.

I had encountered some similar ideas — including side-dock paging and gestures — earlier in an old version of **LINE Launcher**, which I used for a while. Later, I found some of those familiar approaches in TSF Launcher as well. LINE Launcher is part of my memory of how I discovered these interactions, rather than a direct model for this project.

## Desktop effects: Compiz and LibreOffice

The Linux desktop introduced me to effects that made navigating windows and workspaces feel like an experience in its own right.

Some e_e launcher transitions were inspired by **Compiz**, including experimental versions, while others came from the transitions available in **LibreOffice** presentations. Compiz and **Cairo-Dock** also influenced the scaling and bouncing animations used when activating icons.

These references were a starting point rather than a strict specification: I was interested in recreating a familiar feeling while making it work for a phone.

## An aquarium, sunlight, and visual distortions

Aquarium-themed live wallpapers inspired the fish, bubbles, and sun rays in e_e launcher. The appearance of the rays also owes something to the tube-like gradients I had often seen in **GIMP**.

The distortion effects began with memories of aquarium live wallpapers, then picked up elements that reminded me of **Windows 98**. Different influences gradually blended together as I experimented.

## When an experiment takes a different direction

The rain effect began as an attempt to make something like the droplet effects I remembered from Compiz. The result went in another direction, resembling floating messages from **Mega Man Battle Network** and certain visual memories I associate with games and the Mod Archive.

The burning effect also traces back to Compiz. It developed into something unusual enough that I decided not to force it to match the original inspiration.

Those unexpected results became part of the personality of Effects Environment.

## The Scroll effect: Moon+ Reader

The workspace **Scroll** effect grew from an attempt to recreate a page-turning animation I liked in **Moon+ Reader**. The result ended up looking noticeably different from what I had imagined. I kept the experiment as its own variation rather than treating it as a faithful recreation.

## A problem with ColorNote, and the idea of Tasker

The idea for action chains was prompted by a practical problem, not by taking inspiration from ColorNote's design. A behavior I relied on in **ColorNote** — opening the calendar by tapping empty space in a widget — stopped working. I wanted a way to bring that convenience back.

While looking for a more flexible solution, I remembered the idea behind **Tasker**, although I had never used it myself. That led me toward customizable chains of actions rather than restoring only one fixed interaction.

## The bottom dock: Cairo-Dock

The angled, plate-like background beneath the bottom dock was inspired by **Cairo-Dock**. Together with its icon magnification and bouncing animations, it helped shape the dock into something more expressive than a simple row of shortcuts.
